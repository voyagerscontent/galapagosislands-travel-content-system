Dear TBJ,
sorry about your negative experience.
It's important for people reading this to understand that wild places are not zoos, where everything is controlled, safe and made to accommodate even folks in wheelchairs, with animals restricted to small spaces ensuring they can be seen. Sharks move around - they come and go. Seas can be calm, or they can be choppy.
No one can guarantee you'll seen anything at all (within reason). Conditions are variable (Sept-Oct are peak garua season, when seas are at their choppiest).
The odds of witnessing / experiencing amazing displays and phenomenon in Galapagos increase in proportion to the amount of time you spend out there, among the islands (above and below the sea). While a single day trip to one spot, spending 2 hours there is a good start - it's like putting all your money on one bet. You have to spread your time over many days to push the odds in your favour.
Visitors to Galapagos should focus on spending as much time out there as possible. A ship-based experience is best for that (24 hours / day among the islands), following by non-stop day trips, every day of your time in Galapagos. Spending a relaxing day in town is fine, but each day you do that reduces the odds of you having amazing wildlife encounters.
See what these lucky folks got to experience: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FKYLTnyQL3A&

Here are some ideas:
1) Choose a ship with uninterrupted 7 night / 8 day itinerary (not one that is a combination of shorter segments). All ships must follow a 14 night (15 day) circuit, not going back to the same place twice. This is imposed by the Park to spread visitor pressure evenly throughout the archipelago, leading to a better visitor experience and reduced impact. Some ships decide to segment those 14 nights into 3 or 4 short segments, allowing guest to book shorter (ta && ta.queueForLoad ? ta.queueForLoad : function(f, g){document.addEventListener('DOMContentLoaded', f);})(function(){ta.trackEventOnPage('postLinkInline', 'impression', 'postLinks-122490301', '');}, 'log_autolink_impression');cruises (as short as 3 nights). 2) Keep an eye out for activities offered. Some ships have paddleboards, some don't, for example. Some have a reputation for "going all the way" (getting you up at 6AM for a full day of activities) while others will assume you are there more for a relaxing holiday. Decide what's best for you.

## 0. HARD TRUTHS (non-negotiable editorial guardrails)

These override any tempting shortcut in generated copy.

### HT-1 — Timing / seasonality
- **Galápagos is a great year-round destination.** Never state or imply that one time of year is "better," "the best time to go," or "worst" *in general*.
- Timing statements are permitted **only when tied to a specific thing** — a named species, event, or condition — and must say **what it is for**. Example: "April–December is the window to see waved albatross on Española (they nest there and leave by January)." [CDF][GC][GCT]
- **One species must never define the whole destination.** Waved-albatross timing (or any single species) does not make a month better overall — the archipelago always offers abundant wildlife. In any month you can find seabirds displaying, incubating, or feeding young. [GCT][GC]
- You **may** say you *prefer* a season, but only with a **true, specific reason** (e.g. "we like June–November for whale sharks at Darwin & Wolf and richer, cooler seas"). A preference is not a ranking of the destination.
- El Niño years can shift breeding seasons and conditions; calendars are approximate, not guarantees. Never promise a specific animal sighting. [GCT][CDF]

### HT-2 — Islands, cruises & "luxury"
- **No island is better than another.** Every island is attractive and unique (different age, geology, and often single-island endemic species). You **may** say you *prefer* an island, with an explained reason, but that does not make it "better." [CDF][GC][GCT]
- **An island on the itinerary does not make a boat "luxury."** Nearly all boats can visit nearly all islands, so islands visited are not a luxury factor.
- **Luxury depends on the yacht** — its full experience (service, guiding, cuisine, comfort, crew, perks), **not just infrastructure** and not which islands it reaches.
- **Cruise length vs. reach:** all islands can be visited on a **5-day (or longer) cruise**. The **only** sites **not** reachable on a **4-day-or-shorter** program are **Fernandina and the west side of Isabela**. The **shortest cruise is 4 days**, and a 4-day cruise **can** be luxury.
- Do not use "gets to island X" as a proxy for quality or price tier. Compare vessels on their actual experience and transparent criteria.

## 1. Seasonality — species → island(s) → months → what it's FOR

Two broad seasons (labels approximate; sources vary slightly): **warm/wet ~Dec–May** (warmer, calmer, clearer water; best snorkelling; land birds & reptiles breeding; hammerhead schools; mantas) and **cool/dry "garúa" ~Jun–Nov** (cooler, rougher, nutrient-rich seas; best diving; whale sharks; peak seabird feeding). Garúa mist peaks Jul–Sep. [GC][GCT][CDF]

- **Waved albatross — Española only — on land ~Apr–Dec (gone Jan–Mar) — nesting.** Courtship late Mar–Apr, eggs Apr–Jun, chicks fledge by Dec; one egg/year, skips years. World population essentially all on Española. [GCT][GC][CDF]
- **Giant tortoise — Santa Cruz highlands, Alcedo (Isabela) — breed hot season Jan–May; nest/lay cool season ~Jun–Dec — breeding/nesting.** Migrate highland↔lowland; sex temperature-determined (warm=female). [GCT][GC][CDF]
- **Green sea turtle — archipelago-wide beaches (Bartolomé, Las Bachas, Gardner, Quinta Playa, Espumilla) — nesting ~Dec–Mar, hatch ~Apr — nesting.** Only sea turtle that nests in Galápagos. [GCT][GC][CDF]
- **Marine iguana — Isabela, Fernandina, Española, Floreana, Santa Cruz — mating Jan–Mar; young emerge ~Jun — breeding.** Males most colourful ("Christmas iguanas" on Española/Floreana) in breeding season. [GCT][GC]
- **Land iguana — Isabela, Fernandina, Santa Cruz, Baltra, S. Plaza, N. Seymour — mating from Jan, hatch ~Apr — breeding.** [GCT]
- **Blue-footed booby — North Seymour (best), Española, Isabela, Floreana — courtship/nesting ~Jun–Aug — breeding.** [GCT]
- **Nazca booby — Española, Genovesa, San Cristóbal (Punta Pitt) — Genovesa Aug–Nov; Española Nov–Feb — nesting.** [GCT]
- **Red-footed booby — Genovesa (highest concentration), San Cristóbal — ~year-round, viewable at Darwin Bay — nesting in trees.** Smallest booby. [GCT][GC]
- **Frigatebirds (great & magnificent) — North Seymour (largest magnificent colony), Genovesa, Española, San Cristóbal — courtship displays (red pouch) esp. Feb (N. Seymour) & Jun (Genovesa); breed ~year-round.** [GCT][GC]
- **Flightless cormorant — NW Isabela & E Fernandina ONLY — nesting May–Oct — breeding.** ~1,600–2,400 birds; endemic; cannot fly. [GCT][GC][CDF]
- **Galápagos penguin — Fernandina & western Isabela (~95%), also Bartolomé/Floreana/Santiago — breed year-round — nesting.** Only tropical/N-hemisphere penguin; ~2,000–3,000 `[VERIFY]`; El Niño crashes numbers. [GCT][GC][CDF]
- **American flamingo — Floreana (Punta Cormorant), Isabela, Santa Cruz, Rábida, Santiago — lagoons ~Mar–Jul — nesting.** Only ~500–600 in Galápagos; genetically distinct, smaller than Caribbean. [GCT][GC][CDF]
- **Little vermilion flycatcher — Isabela, Fernandina, Rábida, N/NW Santa Cruz Scalesia — breed warm season Dec–May — breeding.** Santa Cruz population critically low (~30); Near Threatened. [GCT][CDF]
- **Whale shark — Darwin & Wolf (dive-only) — ~Jun–Nov (cool season) — feeding aggregation.** Aggregation is unusually ~90% large (likely pregnant) females. [GCT][GC][CDF]
- **Scalloped hammerhead — Darwin, Wolf, also Kicker Rock/Genovesa — schools year-round, peak cool season (~Aug); warm-season pupping migration to mainland — feeding/aggregation.** Critically Endangered, >80% decline. [GCT][GC][CDF]
- **Oceanic manta & rays — Wolf, Darwin, Isabela (Elizabeth Bay), Black Turtle Cove — best ~Dec–May (warmer, clearer) — aggregation.** [GCT]
- **Cetaceans (~24 spp.) — Bolívar Channel (Isabela–Fernandina), best whale-watching — migratory whales mainly Jul–Oct; dolphins/orcas year-round — feeding.** West coast richest (Cromwell upwelling). [GCT][GC]
- **Sea lion — archipelago-wide — pupping from ~Aug (mating Jul–Dec) — breeding.** Best pups: Plazas, Santa Fe, San Cristóbal, Española. [GCT]
- **Galápagos fur seal — western islands, James Bay (Santiago), Darwin Bay (Genovesa) — breeding mid-Aug–mid-Nov, best pups Oct — breeding.** A sea lion, not a true seal; prefers cool shaded shores. [GCT][GC]
- **Surfing (San Cristóbal) — start of warm/wet season — warmer water.** [GC]

## 2. Islands — each is distinct (supports HT-2)

Every island differs in age (older east → youngest west), geology, and endemics; many species are single-island endemics, so no island is redundant. [CDF][GC][GCT]

- **Baltra** — main airport; flat, arid; Navy/Air Force base; no visitor sites of its own; land iguanas went extinct here (~1954), later reintroduced. [GC][GCT]
- **Bartolomé** — iconic Pinnacle Rock; most-photographed viewpoint; snorkel with penguins. [GC][GCT]
- **Daphne Major** — permit-only; site of the Grants' 40+ year finch-evolution study. [GCT][GC][CDF]
- **Darwin & Wolf** — far north; **no land visitor sites — dive-only, liveaboard only**; whale sharks & hammerhead schools; world's largest recorded shark biomass; Darwin hosts the last structural coral reef (Wellington Reef); Wolf has the blood-drinking vampire finch. Darwin's Arch collapsed May 2021 ("Darwin's Pillars"). [CDF][GC][GCT]
- **Española** — southernmost & one of the oldest (~4 M yr); sole world breeding site of the waved albatross; "Christmas iguanas"; tortoises recovered from ~14 to ~2,000. Sites: Punta Suárez, Gardner Bay. [CDF][GC][GCT]
- **Fernandina** — youngest & most volcanically active; among the most pristine (never had introduced mammals); largest/densest marine & land iguanas; single visitor site Punta Espinoza. [GC][GCT][CDF]
- **Floreana** — richest human history (1793 barrel "post office"; 1930s mystery); ~100–160 residents; most human-altered; major ecological-restoration target. Sites: Cormorant Point, Post Office Bay, Devil's Crown. [GC][GCT][CDF]
- **Genovesa** — "the bird island"; horseshoe caldera; highest red-footed booby concentration; never had introduced mammals. Sites: Prince Philip's Steps, Darwin Bay. [GC][GCT]
- **Isabela** — largest island (bigger than all others combined); seahorse-shaped; 6 shield volcanoes; **Wolf Volcano ~1,707 m is the archipelago's highest point**; Sierra Negra caldera among the world's largest; separate tortoise species per volcano; only home of pink land iguana & mangrove finch; Bolívar Channel best cetaceans. Town: Puerto Villamil. [GC][GCT][CDF]
- **Marchena / Pinta** — no tourist landing sites; Pinta was Lonesome George's home (last Pinta tortoise, died Jun 2012). [GC][GCT]
- **North Seymour** — largest magnificent-frigatebird colony; prime seabird breeding. [GC][GCT]
- **Rábida** — distinctive red iron-rich beaches; flamingo lagoon; pelican nesting. [GC][GCT]
- **San Cristóbal** — easternmost; provincial capital Puerto Baquerizo Moreno (~5,400); second airport; where Darwin first landed (1835); **El Junco = only permanent freshwater lake**; Punta Pitt is the only place all three booby species nest together; surfing hub; Kicker Rock (León Dormido) dive site. [GC][GCT][CDF]
- **Santa Cruz** — 2nd largest; central; largest town Puerto Ayora (~12,000+); main tourism hub; Charles Darwin Research Station & park HQ; the island where highlands are readily visited (Los Gemelos, tortoise ranches, lava tubes, Tortuga Bay). [GC][GCT][CDF]
- **Santa Fe** — among oldest islands (~3.9 M yr); giant Opuntia; endemic land iguana & rice rat. [GC][GCT][CDF]
- **Santiago** — uninhabited; recent black lava flows (Sullivan Bay); best fur-seal viewing (Puerto Egas); land iguanas reintroduced 2019. [GC][GCT][CDF]
- **South Plaza** — smallest land iguanas; the only place land & marine iguanas interbreed; red Sesuvium. (North Plaza is research-only/closed.) [GC][GCT]

## 3. Visitor sites & access (writer logic)

- All National-Park visitor sites require a **GNPS/park-licensed naturalist guide**; only a handful near towns (e.g. Tortuga Bay, Las Grietas, some town beaches) can be visited unguided. [GC][GCT][CDF]
- **Day-trip vs cruise:** day-trips from inhabited islands reach roughly **one-third** of sites (near Santa Cruz, San Cristóbal, Isabela, Floreana). **Western sites (Fernandina, west Isabela — Elizabeth Bay, Urbina Bay, Tagus Cove, Punta Vicente Roca) require a multi-day cruise.** This is the factual basis of HT-2 (Fernandina & west Isabela not reachable on ≤4-day cruises). [GC][GCT]
- **Darwin & Wolf are liveaboard-dive-only** (no land sites); reachable on ~7-day dive liveaboards. [CDF][GC][GCT]
- **Permit-restricted / closed:** Daphne Major, Alcedo Volcano, North Plaza, mangrove-finch sites (NW Isabela), pink-iguana range (Wolf Volcano). [GC][GCT][CDF]
- The authoritative per-site permitted-activity list (210 sites, marine vs terrestrial, activities like snorkel/dive/kayak/panga/walk/camp) is the **DPNG visitor-sites dataset** — see the workbook sheets sourced from the DPNG/FCD ArcGIS layer. Use it to check what activities are allowed at a named site; never invent an activity for a site.

## 4. Conservation & science (for depth; keep neutral, cite)

- ~97% of land is protected National Park (est. 1959); first UNESCO World Heritage Site (1978); Biosphere Reserve (1984). [CDF][GC][GCT]
- Galápagos Marine Reserve established **1998 (~138,000 km²)**; Hermandad/Swimway reserve added **60,000 km² in 2022** (migratory shark/turtle corridor toward Cocos). Marine-reserve area cited as 133,000–138,000 km² `[VERIFY]`. [CDF][GC][GCT]
- Endemism is exceptionally high: ~80% of land birds; ~97% of reptiles/land mammals; >1,300 endemic species overall. The islands retain all their native bird species (rare for an oceanic archipelago). [GCT][GC]
- **Giant tortoises:** ~12 living species (of ~15 described), all threatened; historical population 200,000–300,000, now ~20,000–25,000; captive breeding since 1965 has returned 7,000–8,000+ to the wild; Española recovered from ~14–15 animals. Lonesome George (last Pinta tortoise) died Jun 2012. [CDF][GC][GCT]
- **Iguanas:** 4 species incl. the **pink land iguana** (Wolf Volcano only, ~350, Critically Endangered) and the marine iguana (only sea-feeding lizard). [GC][GCT]
- **Rarest bird = mangrove finch** (~20–100 individuals `[VERIFY]`, NW Isabela only) — not the tortoise. [CDF][GC]
- **Top threats:** invasive species (black rats; feral goats/pigs/donkeys; cats/dogs; fire ants), the introduced avian vampire fly *Philornis downsi* (kills nestlings), invasive plants (blackberry/mora, guava), plastic pollution (mostly from outside the reserve), overfishing/shark-finning, and El Niño (crashes penguins, cormorants, marine iguanas, corals). [CDF][GC][GCT]
- Landmark wins: Project Isabela/Pinta goat & pig eradications (by ~2006); rat eradications (Pinzón 2012, Rábida, North Seymour); Floreana restoration (tortoise reintroduction from 2024–2026; Galápagos rail rediscovered on Floreana 2025). [CDF][GC][GCT]
- Galápagos is **not a coral-reef destination** — reefs are naturally scarce and were ~97% reduced in the 1982–83 El Niño; only Wellington Reef (Darwin) remains structural. Highland "Scalesia forest" is giant-daisy woodland, not rainforest (~1% remains). [CDF]

## 5. Practical / logistics (stable facts)

- **Getting there:** flights from Quito or Guayaquil (~3 hrs) to **Baltra** (most traffic) or **San Cristóbal**; no passenger boats from the mainland. Arrive a day early. [CDF][GC][GCT]
- **National Park entry fee: US$200** for most foreign adults (raised 1 Aug 2024; first rise since 1998); reduced rates for children, Ecuadorians/CAN-MERCOSUR; paid in cash on arrival. [GCT][CDF]
- **Transit Control Card (INGALA/TCT): US$20** per traveller (buy online in advance as of 2025); keep for departure. [GCT][CDF]
- **Biosecurity** enforced by ABG: luggage inspected; no fresh produce/seeds; **no tourist drones**; sniffer-dog teams at Baltra & (2025) San Cristóbal. [GCT][GC][CDF]
- **Park rules:** licensed guide in protected areas; stay **≥2 m** from wildlife; no touching/feeding/flash; stay on trails; take nothing (rocks/shells/sand); no wildlife souvenirs (black coral, shells, turtle shell); reef-safe sunscreen; camping by permit only; very high equatorial UV. [GCT][GC][CDF]
- **Money:** US dollar; ATMs only on Santa Cruz & San Cristóbal (limited on Isabela; none on Floreana — carry small bills); 110 V US plugs. [GCT][CDF]
- **Water is often cold** (Humboldt/Cromwell currents) despite the equator — wetsuits usual; diving here is not for beginners (currents, cold, big animals). [GCT][GC]
- Visitor numbers ~280,000–330,000/yr (record ~2023); tourism is actively managed for sustainability. `[VERIFY]` exact figure/year. [GCT][GC]

## 6. Misconceptions to guard against (writer must not repeat)

- "There is a single best time to visit." (False — see HT-1.)
- "One island is the best." (False — see HT-2.)
- "An itinerary island makes a boat luxury." (False — see HT-2.)
- "You'll arrive to lush tropical jungle." (False — mostly arid; green only in highlands.) [GC][CDF]
- "You can dive Darwin/Wolf on a day trip." (False — liveaboard-dive-only.) [GC][GCT][CDF]
- "Darwin discovered Galápagos / theorised evolution there." (False — recorded 1535 by Berlanga; Darwin visited 1835 for ~5 weeks; the insight came later.) [CDF][GC][GCT]
- "The Galápagos shark is endemic." (False — found across oceans.) [GCT]
- "Whale sharks are whales." (False — largest fish; here mostly pregnant females.) [GCT][GC]
- "A fur seal is a seal." (It's a sea lion.) [GC]
- "North Seymour land iguanas are native." (Introduced from Baltra in the 1930s.) [GC]
- "Lonesome George was the last tortoise." (Last *Pinta* tortoise; 12 species remain.) [CDF][GC]
- "The waters are warm/tropical." (Often cold — bring a wetsuit.) [GCT]
- Never conflate the **Charles Darwin Foundation** (science NGO) with the **Galápagos National Park Directorate/GNPS** (the park authority). [CDF]

The short answer

Plan on roughly $250–$400 per person per day for a budget, land-based trip; $500–$900/day for a mid-range cruise; and $1,000–$2,500+/day for a high-end yacht. On top of that, everyone pays a $200 park fee, a ~$20 transit card, and ~$400–$500 for the mainland flight.

The fixed costs everyone pays

Some line items are the same whether you backpack or book a private yacht:

  • Galápagos National Park entrance fee — $200 per adult for most foreign visitors, cash on arrival. Children and some nationalities pay less. Set by the Galápagos National Park Directorate (DPNG). [source: DPNG]
  • Ingala Transit Control Card — ~$20, bought at the mainland airport before you fly.
  • Round-trip mainland flight — ~$400–$500 from Quito or Guayaquil; about 3 hours from Quito.

Before your first island night, budget around $620–$720 per person for fees and the flight. Spread across a week, that's about $90–$100 per day. Figures are indicative through early 2026 — [VERIFY current rates].

Cruise vs. land-based: the big fork

Your biggest budget decision isn't which islands you see — it's how you sleep.

Cruise (live-aboard yacht): you sail overnight and wake at a new site, which is how you reach the most remote landings. Daily rates run about $500–$900 for tourist- and first-class boats and $1,000–$2,500+ for the top yachts.

Land-based (hotel + day tours): you stay in the island towns — Puerto Ayora, Puerto Baquerizo Moreno, or Puerto Villamil — and day-boat to nearby sites. About $250–$450/day all-in for budget to mid-range travelers.

An honest trade-off, not a ranking: land-based is easier on the wallet; a cruise reaches sites day boats can't, especially the western islands. Neither is "better" — they answer different questions.

What "luxury" actually costs — and what it doesn't

A common myth is that paying more buys you better islands. It doesn't. Nearly every vessel visits the headline islands, and every island is reachable on a cruise of 5 days or longer. The only sites you'll miss on a 4-day-or-shorter program are Fernandina and the west coast of Isabela — a function of itinerary length, not price.

What the premium actually buys is the yacht experience: more space per guest, better cabins and food, a higher crew-to-guest ratio, top naturalist guides, and gear like kayaks. A 4-day cruise can be genuinely high-end; a longer cruise can be modest. Luxury is the boat, not the map.

Timing and your budget

You can travel any month and have a great trip; the wildlife calendar shifts, not the quality. Prices firm up around the mid-June–August and mid-December–early-January holiday peaks, so shoulder weeks on either side often book cheaper. Choose your month for what you want to see — not for a single "best" time.

Five honest ways to spend less

  • Fly from Guayaquil rather than Quito when you can — closer, sometimes cheaper.
  • Pair a shorter cruise with a couple of land-based days instead of one long sail.
  • Travel shoulder weeks; book last-minute cruise space if your dates are flexible.
  • Base on one island and use day boats rather than changing hotels.
  • Bring cash — the park fee is cash-only and island ATMs can run dry.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a Galápagos trip cost per day?

Budget on about $250–$400 per person per day land-based, $500–$900 for a mid-range cruise, and $1,000–$2,500+ for a high-end yacht. Fixed fees and the island flight add roughly $90–$100 per day across a week.

Is the Galápagos National Park fee really $200?

For most foreign adults, yes — the fee rose to $200 in 2024. It's paid in cash on arrival; children and some nationalities pay less. Confirm the current rate before you travel.

Can I do the Galápagos on a budget?

Yes. A land-based trip using town hotels and day tours can run around $250–$300 per person per day, before international flights, if you keep meals simple and book day boats locally.

Does a more expensive cruise visit better islands?

No. Almost all boats visit the same headline islands; only Fernandina and western Isabela require an itinerary of 5 days or more, regardless of price. A higher price buys a better on-board experience, not better islands.

What's not included in a Galápagos cruise fare?

International flights to Ecuador, the $200 park fee and ~$20 transit card, travel insurance, tips (often $10–$25/day), some alcohol and wetsuit rental, and a night or two in Quito or Guayaquil around your island flight.

What is the cheapest month to visit the Galápagos?

The islands are a year-round destination, so quality doesn't track a single month. Prices firm up over the mid-June–August and mid-December–early-January peaks, so shoulder weeks on either side often book cheaper.
